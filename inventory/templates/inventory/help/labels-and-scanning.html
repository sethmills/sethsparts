Why scanning beats searching

Searching needs you to remember a name and spell it the way you did last time. Scanning needs you to be holding the thing.

Every container, drawer and bin can carry a barcode. Scan one and you go straight to it — what's inside, what's in each bin, and what to add. This is the fastest path to an answer once the barcodes are on.

Printing labels

Labels are drawn by the app and sent to the print bridge described under Lights and the label printer. There are three sizes defined by default — a general 4″×2″, a tote 2″×1″ and a small 1″×0.5″ barcode label.

A label can carry free text, a barcode, or both, at whatever size and weight you like. The custom label page shows a preview before you commit it to paper.

Put the barcode on before the thing goes in the drawer. Barcoding afterwards means taking it all out again.

Barcoding a cabinet of drawers

The bin barcodes page walks the whole cabinet one bin at a time, so you print as you go rather than trying to plan a sheet of labels up front. It remembers where you got to.

If a code is already in use elsewhere, the app tells you where, and refuses to silently reassign it — a barcode pointing at two places is worse than no barcode.

The scan box

Paste or type a code into the scan page and it resolves it: a container takes you to that container, a drawer to that drawer, a bin to its position within the drawer, and a part code to that part.

Unknown codes say so plainly rather than guessing at a near match.

A phone works too

Nothing here needs special hardware. A phone camera and a barcode app, or a cheap USB scanner, both feed the same box.
