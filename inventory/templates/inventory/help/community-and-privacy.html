The three separate things

They sound like one feature and are deliberately three, because collapsing them is how a privacy model breaks:

  1. Appearing on the map. Off by default. A pin at district level, with no name unless you set one.
  2. Exchanging map pins with a workshop you've connected to. On by default for the one connection every install starts with. This never gives anyone your inventory.
  3. Being searchable. Off until you turn it on for one specific person, and even then only for the categories you tick.

Connecting with someone for the map can never hand them your parts. If that ever stops being true, it's a bug.

What your location actually is

A district — the outward part of a UK postcode like SW1A, or a US ZIP. Never a full postcode, never a street, never a point you're standing in.

This matters more than it sounds: a full UK postcode identifies roughly fifteen households, so publishing one publishes a doorstep. Even if you type your full postcode, the app reduces it to the district before storing anything.

There's no rounding or fuzzing step, because there's nothing to fuzz — the district is what comes back from the lookup.

There is no central server

Nothing about the community feature lives on a server anyone runs on your behalf. Your instance asks the workshops you're connected to whether they know anyone nearby, and they pass on what they know — but only when you ask it to: the button on the map, or manage.py sync_community_pins on a timer. Simply opening the map contacts nobody.

Pins are signed by whoever published them, which is what lets a workshop pass one on without being able to move it, rename it, or invent one that isn't theirs.

That's why one connection is enough to start seeing a map, and why connecting to any other workshop keeps it working if one goes away.

Every install starts connected to the app's maintainer, so a brand-new instance has somewhere to ask. That connection is pins only, and you can remove it.

Turning it off, properly off

Untick show my workshop as a pin and the pin goes. What gets published afterwards is a signed “I'm gone” note, which every instance that sees it deletes rather than keeps — so an opt-out genuinely removes the data rather than hiding it.

Revoke a connection and it stops being asked anything, immediately.

This is the reason the app signs entries instead of using a blockchain. An append-only ledger cannot honour “delete my data” — the entry would survive on every node forever, which is the opposite of what an opt-out has to mean.

What other people can see

On the map, before you're connected: an anonymous pin. Nothing else.

After you connect: whatever you've chosen to share — your workshop's display name, if you set one, and the categories you've ticked for that person.

Every search a connected workshop makes against your inventory is logged, and you can read that log. So “they can only search what I shared” is something you can check rather than something you have to take on trust.
